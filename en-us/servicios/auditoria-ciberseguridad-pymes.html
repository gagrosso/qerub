The problem

Most SMBs have no idea how attackers can get in.

96% of breaches at companies with 10 to 250 employees come through three paths: system intrusion, social engineering and basic web attacks (Verizon DBIR 2025). Ransomware shows up in 88% of incidents in this segment. You don't need a sophisticated attacker: a leaked password, an open mail-forwarding rule or a backup that was never tested is enough.

Traditional audits are built for large enterprises: weeks of interviews, encyclopedic reports and generic recommendations. An SMB needs the opposite: a short, prioritized diagnosis with a fixed price that leadership can understand and approve without an in-house technical team.

What's included

We review the six fronts where incidents are decided.

Email and domain

SPF, DKIM, DMARC, forwarding rules, domain exposure and anti-spoofing configuration.

Identity and access

MFA, privileged accounts, passwords leaked in public breaches and former-employee access.

Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace

Tenant configuration, Conditional Access, connected apps and excessive permissions.

Public web and TLS

Certificates, security headers, exposed versions and the visible attack surface.

Backups

What gets backed up, where, how often and — most important — whether a restore has actually been tested.

Incident response

Whether a plan exists, who decides, who gets called and how long it would take you to get back up and running.
