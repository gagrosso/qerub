The problem

Email fraud doesn't exploit a technical flaw. It exploits trust.

Business Email Compromise (BEC) — CEO fraud, vendor fraud, bank-account changes — is the cybercrime category with the largest direct financial losses for small and midsize businesses. It needs no malware: a lookalike domain, one compromised real email thread, and a wire approved in a hurry are enough.

On top of that, without correct SPF, DKIM and DMARC, anyone can send email "from" your domain — to your customers, your accountant, your bank. And since 2024, Google and Yahoo reject or downgrade mail from domains without authentication: your own legitimate emails end up in spam.

What's included

Three layers: authentication, visible signals, and procedure.

Correct SPF

A real inventory of who sends on your behalf (CRM, billing, marketing) and a record with no errors or dangerous "+all".

DKIM on every sender

Active signatures in Microsoft 365 / Google and on every platform that sends in your name.

DMARC with an active policy

From p=none to p=quarantine/reject gradually and under monitoring, without losing legitimate mail.

External email banners

A visible warning on mail coming from outside — the simplest antidote to internal impersonation.

Dual payment approval

A written procedure: every bank-account change is verified by phone to a number you already know.

What to do in the first 60 minutes if fraud is detected: bank, evidence, report.

Frequently asked questions

Before you hire us

Doesn't my email provider already handle this?

Microsoft and Google sign their own mail, but they won't discover your external senders or publish your DMARC policy. That's the domain owner's responsibility.

Can I lose legitimate mail when I turn on DMARC?

That's why we do it in phases: monitoring first (p=none), then gradual quarantine. We never move to reject without weeks of clean data.

Does it include team training?

It includes a short session for finance and treasury on the dual-approval procedure. Ongoing training is Q-Phishing Test.
