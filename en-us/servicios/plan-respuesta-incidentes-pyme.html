The problem

The backups exist. Nobody knows whether they work, or who decides at 3 a.m.

Ransomware shows up in 88% of breaches affecting SMBs (Verizon DBIR 2025). And the pattern repeats: the backups were never tested, there's no printed contact list (it was on the encrypted server), nobody knows whether they can pay or not, and days are lost on decisions that could have been made in advance.

An incident response plan isn't a 60-page document: it's a decision sheet, clear roles, and two or three rehearsed procedures. The difference between a company with a plan and one without is measured in days of downtime — and in whether the incident turns into a public crisis.

Frequently asked questions

Before you sign on

How is this different from a full business continuity plan?

It's the essential, actionable version: it covers the decision and the first 72 hours. A full BCP (ISO 22301) can be built later on top of this foundation.

Do we need to shut down systems for the backup test?

No. We restore to an isolated test environment. Your operations are never touched.

How often is the plan updated?

We recommend an annual review or one after major changes (systems, key people). With Q-Continuity, the drill is repeated every year within your retainer.
